Lecture 1 — Membrane transport
Cell Biology (demo course) · Week 1


1.1 The plasma membrane

The plasma membrane is a phospholipid bilayer with embedded proteins (the fluid
mosaic model). Its hydrophobic core lets small non-polar molecules (O2, CO2) pass,
but blocks ions and large polar molecules such as glucose. The membrane is
therefore selectively permeable.

1.2 Passive transport

Passive transport needs no energy from the cell: substances move down their
concentration gradient, from high to low concentration, until equilibrium.

      Simple diffusion — directly through the bilayer (O2, CO2, steroid hormones).
      Facilitated diffusion — through channel proteins or carrier proteins (glucose
      via GLUT transporters, ions via ion channels). Still passive: no ATP is used.

The rate of diffusion increases with a steeper gradient, higher temperature and a
larger surface area, and decreases with a longer diffusion distance.

1.3 Osmosis

Osmosis is the passive movement of water across a selectively permeable
membrane, from a region of lower solute concentration to a region of higher solute
concentration. Many cells speed it up with water channels called aquaporins.

1.4 Active transport

Active transport moves substances against their concentration gradient, using
energy, usually from ATP. It needs specific carrier proteins called pumps.

Key example — the sodium–potassium pump (Na+/K+-ATPase): for each ATP
hydrolysed it moves 3 Na+ out of the cell and 2 K+ in. This keeps the inside of the
cell negative and maintains the resting membrane potential of neurons.

1.5 Bulk transport

     Endocytosis — the membrane folds inwards to take material in: phagocytosis
     ("cell eating", e.g. a white blood cell engulfing bacteria) and pinocytosis ("cell
     drinking").
     Exocytosis — vesicles fuse with the membrane to release their contents (e.g.
     neurotransmitters, hormones).

Both use ATP, so they count as active processes.

Summary

Passive (diffusion, facilitated diffusion, osmosis): down the gradient, no ATP. Active
(pumps, endo- and exocytosis): against the gradient or in bulk, needs ATP.
