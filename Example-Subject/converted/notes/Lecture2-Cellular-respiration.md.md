# Lecture 2 — Cellular respiration

Cell Biology (demo course) · Week 2 · my notes from the lecture

## Overview

Cellular respiration releases the energy stored in glucose and stores it as ATP.
Overall: C6H12O6 + 6 O2 → 6 CO2 + 6 H2O + energy (about 30–32 ATP per glucose).

Four stages, in this order:

1. Glycolysis — cytoplasm
2. Link reaction (pyruvate oxidation) — mitochondrial matrix
3. Krebs cycle (citric acid cycle) — mitochondrial matrix
4. Electron transport chain + oxidative phosphorylation — inner mitochondrial membrane

## 1. Glycolysis

- Glucose (6C) is split into 2 pyruvate (3C).
- Does not need oxygen.
- Uses 2 ATP, makes 4 ATP → net gain **2 ATP**, plus **2 NADH**.

## 2. Link reaction

- Each pyruvate enters the mitochondrion and becomes acetyl-CoA (2C).
- Releases 1 CO2 and makes 1 NADH per pyruvate (so 2 CO2 + 2 NADH per glucose).

## 3. Krebs cycle

- Acetyl-CoA (2C) joins oxaloacetate (4C) to form citrate (6C); the cycle regenerates oxaloacetate.
- Per turn: **3 NADH, 1 FADH2, 1 ATP (or GTP), 2 CO2**.
- The cycle turns **twice per glucose** (one turn per acetyl-CoA) — lecturer said this is the most common
  exam trap: double the per-turn numbers when asked "per glucose".

## 4. Electron transport chain (ETC)

- NADH and FADH2 hand their electrons to protein complexes in the inner membrane.
- The energy pumps H+ ions into the intermembrane space → a proton gradient.
- H+ flows back through **ATP synthase**, which makes most of the ATP (chemiosmosis).
- **Oxygen is the final electron acceptor** and forms water. Without O2 the chain stops.

## Without oxygen: fermentation

- Only glycolysis runs, so only 2 ATP per glucose.
- Fermentation regenerates NAD+ so glycolysis can continue:
  - animals: pyruvate → lactate (lactic acid fermentation, e.g. muscles during a sprint)
  - yeast: pyruvate → ethanol + CO2 (alcoholic fermentation)

## Lecturer's summary

ATP per glucose: glycolysis 2 + Krebs 2 + oxidative phosphorylation ~26–28 ≈ 30–32 in total.
